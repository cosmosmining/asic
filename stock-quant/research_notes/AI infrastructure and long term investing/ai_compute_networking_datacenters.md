## November 2026 selloff — causes and status

### Takeaway
November's drawdown came from several separate shocks hitting an extended, crowded AI trade. The demand numbers reported during the selloff were not weak.
- **Election repricing:** The Nov 3 midterm "blue wave" led investors to reprice AI capex and data-center policy. The SOX fell 12.25% on Nov 5, its worst day since March 2020.
- **AI credit stress:** CoreWeave bond yields rose above 16% (Nov 6), and the Fed's Financial Stability Report flagged $3T of off-balance-sheet AI commitments (Nov 17).
- **Nvidia governance, plus new export curbs:** Huang resigned on Nov 12, new chip curbs came on Nov 16, and the board refused to reinstate him on Nov 17. It then reinstated him on Nov 23, and NVDA rose 31.5%.
- **Macro:** A hot inflation print and new tariffs hit on Nov 25.
- **Fundamentals stayed strong:** Nvidia reported $115.8B with a $130B guide (Nov 17), and Dell reported a $132.3B AI backlog (Nov 24).

The trigger for the episode's low, **Nov 20 (NVDA −14.0%, SPY −3.6%), was not identified in any source retrieved.**

Status on Nov 25: NVDA closed at $149.30, ≈ −38% from its Oct 6 peak and ≈ −20% YTD.

## Q1. AI capex: 2026 spend and 2027 guidance (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, ORCL), OpenAI/Stargate, sovereign AI, total-spend estimates, and over-build / financing-stress signals

### Takeaway
The big four hyperscalers are guiding about $720–740B of 2026 capex, and every one of them says 2027 will be higher. Street 2027 estimates run from ~$1.2T (Goldman, Sept 25) to $1.32–1.6T (Morgan Stanley, Nov 8), and management everywhere still says demand exceeds supply. The stress is in how the build-out is paid for, not in demand. Big Tech free cash flow has turned negative. Off-balance-sheet leases and SPVs now exceed $3T (Fed, Nov 2026). Oracle and CoreWeave credit spreads have blown out, GPU-backed lending has grown 7x, and there is circular vendor financing around OpenAI. November 2026 added a policy and governance shock: a midterm "blue wave" (Nov 3), the SOX's −12% day (Nov 5), new chip curbs (Nov 16), and Nvidia's CEO resigning (Nov 12) then being reinstated (Nov 23).

### Inferences
- **2026 spend:** The big four plus Oracle come to roughly **$810–840B** of 2026–FY27 capex. That is my arithmetic: $720–740B for the big four plus ~$90–95B for Oracle (Oracle's FY27 figure is from one analysis, not company guidance). It is consistent with Goldman's "~$800B this year."
- **2027 growth:** Street estimates imply **+30% to +60%** growth in 2027, depending on scope. MS's +29% tracker and GS's $1.2T sit at the low end; MS's $1.32T five-company exhibit sits at the high end. The spread itself is a sign of uncertainty.
- **Over-build is not yet visible in demand indicators.** Microsoft, Dell, ASML, SK hynix, Samsung and Cisco all say demand exceeds supply. The binding constraints are third-party data-center deliveries (MSFT, NBIS, IREN), memory, lasers/DSPs and CoWoS.
- **The stress is in the financing chain:**
  - Hyperscaler free cash flow is negative or shrinking.
  - Off-balance-sheet leases (MSFT alone has $329B uncommenced) and SPVs (Hyperion) are growing.
  - Vendor guarantees (NVDA up to $105B to SB Energy) and warrants (AMD) tie suppliers to their customers.
  - High-yield neoclouds (CRWV >16% bond yield) and Oracle (BBB-) sit in the middle, and miners/developers are funded by project debt and converts.
- **The weakest links** are levered intermediaries whose revenue rests on a few AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) whose funding in turn depends on capital markets.
- **Policy risk rose sharply in November.** The midterm result and electricity-cost politics, Asia chip curbs, and Nvidia's governance turmoil all hit at once. Investors should expect more volatility (34 daily moves over 4% in chip ETFs is cited elsewhere) and should not read it as a change in fundamentals by itself.
- **Depreciation lever:** Lengthening asset lives (MSFT buildings 15→25 years) flatters near-term earnings and reported capex. A reversal, like Amazon's partial 6→5-year cut, would hit EPS. This is a key debate for 2027.

## Q3. "Next topic" sub-themes: which got rising investor attention in H2 2026, and the purest listed exposure

### Takeaway
In H2 2026, attention went most clearly to photonics/CPO/1.6T optics, the memory super-cycle and HBM4 pricing, and custom ASICs. Evidence: dedicated photonics, memory and neocloud ETF launches (Aug 2026), analysts raising forecasts by large steps, and the largest stock moves. Scale-up networking (UALink/ESUN vs NVLink) and inference-specific silicon are the next emerging debates. Advanced packaging and liquid cooling are steady "picks and shovels" with fewer pure plays. The purest listed exposures:
- **CPO/1.6T:** LITE, COHR, FN, AAOI (speculative), CRDO.
