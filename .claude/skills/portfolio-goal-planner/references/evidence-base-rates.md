# Evidence and base rates for long-term investing

Cite these when making long-run claims. Tags: [peer-reviewed], [working paper],
[industry], [calc] = computed from public data.

## Index returns and big multiples

- S&P 500 total return 1926–2023 compounded about **10.2% a year nominal and 7.0%
  real** [calc, Shiller monthly data:
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datasets/s-and-p-500/main/data/data.csv].
- Share of holding periods since 1926 in which the index itself returned **50x or
  more** (nominal): 20 years 0%, 30 years 1.2%, 40 years 72.5%, 50 years 96.5%.
  After inflation: 40 years 0%, 50 years 8.6% [calc, same data]. Typical 40-year
  multiple about 65x nominal and 13x real.
- Return needed for 50x: 47.9% a year for 10 years, 29.8% for 15, 21.6% for 20,
  16.9% for 25, 13.9% for 30, 10.3% for 40. The best 20-year S&P window ever was
  about 17.9% a year.
- Forecasts for the next decade are lower than history because of high starting
  valuations. J.P. Morgan's 2027 long-term assumptions (Oct 2026) put US large caps
  at 7.5% a year. Vanguard (Dec 2025) said 4–5%. GMO (Nov 2025) forecast negative
  real returns for US large caps. Use 6–8% nominal as a cautious planning case.

## Single stocks

- Bessembinder (2018, Journal of Financial Economics), "Do stocks outperform
  Treasury bills?": about 57% of US stocks lost to one-month T-bills over their
  lives, and about 4% of companies created all net stock-market wealth from 1926 to
  2016 [peer-reviewed].
- Bessembinder et al. (2023, Financial Analysts Journal), 64,000 global stocks
  1990–2020: 55% of US and 57% of non-US stocks lagged T-bills, and 2.4% of firms
  created all net wealth [peer-reviewed].
- Bessembinder (2024, SSRN 4897069): 51.6% of US stocks had negative lifetime
  returns from 1926 to 2023. The 17 best long-lived stocks averaged about 13.5% a
  year, so even they needed about 31 years for 50x [working paper].
- J.P. Morgan, "The Agony & the Ecstasy" (2021): over 40% of Russell 3000 stocks
  since 1980 fell 70%+ and never recovered [industry].
- Mayer, *100 Baggers* (2015), and Phelps, *100 to 1* (1972): hundred-baggers took
  about 26 years on average, about 19% a year.

## Volatility drag and sizing

- The median outcome compounds at roughly mean minus half the variance. A stock
  with a 15% expected return and 45% volatility has a mean 30-year multiple of about
  66x but a **median of 3.2x**, about a 13% chance of 50x and about a 32% chance of
  losing money [calc, lognormal].
- Kelly sizing: full Kelly carries about a 50% chance of halving at some point, and
  half Kelly about 12.5%. Use a quarter to half Kelly at most, because estimated
  edges are usually too optimistic.
- Eventual mega-winners fell hard: Amazon −94% (2000–01), Nvidia −90%, −85% and
  −66%, Apple −82%, Microsoft −69% and under water for a decade. Cisco took until
  Dec 2025 to regain its 2000 peak.

## Themes and boom-era suppliers

- Ben-David, Franzoni, Kim and Moussawi (2023, Review of Financial Studies),
  "Competition for Attention in the ETF Space": specialized ETFs launch when their
  holdings are overvalued and then underperform [peer-reviewed].
- Morningstar Global Thematic Funds Landscape (2024): only 9% of thematic funds
  survived and beat global equities over 15 years, and 60% closed [industry].
- Telecom boom 1996–2002: only about 3–5% of the fiber laid was lit. The four
  biggest equipment makers fell from over $700bn to about $11bn by 2002. UK
  railways in the 1840s: investment about 7% of GDP, dividends about 1.8% by 1850.
  The infrastructure got built; many builders' shareholders lost.

## Strategies with evidence

- Cross-sectional momentum: Jegadeesh and Titman (1993, Journal of Finance).
- Time-series momentum: Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012, Journal of Financial
  Economics).
- 10-month moving-average trend filter: Faber (2007). On S&P data 1926–2023 it cut
  the worst drawdown from about −82% to about −46% to −51% at a cost of 0–1.6
  points a year [calc].
- Quality and profitability: Novy-Marx (2013, JFE); Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen,
  "Quality Minus Junk" (2019, Review of Accounting Studies).
- Momentum crashes: Daniel and Moskowitz (2016, JFE). Momentum can lose 50–90% in
  sharp rebounds after bear markets, so cap the weight of momentum sleeves.
- Lump sum vs gradual investing (Vanguard): a lump sum won about two thirds of the
  time [industry].
- Costs: a 1% yearly fee turns a 45x 40-year result into about 31x. Taxing gains
  every year at 20% (instead of deferring) turns it into about 22x versus about 36x
  [calc].

## How to use these

State the base rate first. Then explain why this case might differ, and what
evidence would show it is different.
