# Allocation playbook

Illustrative frameworks, not personal advice. Adjust for taxes, existing holdings
and the user's real drawdown tolerance.

## Pick a profile from horizon and loss tolerance

| Profile | Horizon | Tolerable worst drop | Core equity | Satellite | Bonds/T-bills |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady compounder | 10+ yrs | about 30% | 80–90% (US total market + international) | 0–5% | 5–20% |
| Growth tilt | 15+ yrs | about 40% | 70–80% (incl. 15–20% Nasdaq-100 or growth) | 10–15% | 0–10% |
| Aggressive | 20+ yrs, steady income | 50%+ | 55–70% | 20–30% | 0–5% |

Money needed within about 5 years does not belong in stocks.

## Example building blocks (check current fees before use)

- **US total market or S&P 500**: VTI, VOO, IVV, SPYM (about 0.02–0.03%).
- **International**: VXUS (developed + emerging), or VEA + VWO.
- **Growth tilt**: QQQM (Nasdaq-100, cheaper share class of QQQ), VUG or SCHG.
- **Factor tilts with evidence**: small-cap value (AVUV), quality (QUAL), dividend
  growth (VIG, SCHD). They diversify a mega-cap-heavy index.
- **Ballast**: total bond (BND, AGG), T-bills (SGOV, BIL) for cash and dry powder.
- **Themes for satellites**: semiconductors (SMH, SOXX), grid and electrification
  (GRID, PAVE), nuclear (NLR), data-center supply chain (newer funds such as RACK or
  AIPO, which lack long records). Check expense ratio, holdings and overlap with
  the core. Thematic funds launched near hype peaks have a poor record, so prefer
  broad, cheap ones or a hand-built basket of profitable leaders.

## Satellite rules

1. All satellites together at 10–30% of the portfolio, sized so that a 70% loss in
   them still leaves the goal reachable.
2. Single stocks at 2–5% each, speculative or pre-revenue names at 1% or less.
3. Treat names that move together as one position. Several AI-infrastructure stocks
   with 0.6+ correlation are one bet.
4. Enter in stages (thirds over a few months). For new satellite money after a
   crash, a simple trigger such as price back above its 200-day average avoids
   catching most falling knives.
5. Rebalance yearly, or when a satellite reaches 1.5x its target weight: sell the
   excess into the core.
6. Write kill criteria for each theme: evidence that ends the thesis.

## Core rules

- Automate contributions on a schedule; keep contributing in crashes.
- Rebalance once a year or at ±5-point bands.
- Lump sum beat dollar-cost averaging about two thirds of the time historically.
  Spreading over 3–12 months is fine if it prevents regret-driven mistakes.
- Use tax-advantaged accounts first, hold high-turnover strategies there, and keep
  costs low.
- Avoid leverage (margin, leveraged ETFs) for long-term goals. A daily 3x Nasdaq-100
  fund would have been wiped out in 2000–2002.

## Behavior

The plan fails mostly through selling in a crash or chasing a hot theme after it
has run. Write down in advance what the user will do when the portfolio is down
30% and when a theme is up 200%.
