# Bubble and hype checklist

Score each item 0 (no), 1 (somewhat) or 2 (clearly), citing a dated fact for each.
A total of 0–6 is ordinary, 7–12 is late-cycle (size smaller, stage entries), and
13–20 is bubble territory (speculative-only sizing, or wait).

| # | Sign | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Price far ahead of fundamentals | Basket up 3x+ in 2–3 years while revenue grew much less. EV/sales above 20, or forward P/E above 60 for leaders |
| 2 | Spending outruns its funding | Capex financed by debt, vendor financing, off-balance-sheet leases or special-purpose vehicles rather than cash flow |
| 3 | Circular deals | Suppliers investing in customers who then buy from the supplier |
| 4 | Pre-revenue darlings | Many listed names with no revenue trading at billions, frequent stock-funded deals and SPAC listings |
| 5 | ETF and IPO launch wave | New thematic ETFs and IPOs clustered in the last 12–18 months (thematic funds launch near peaks) |
| 6 | Narrative over numbers | Stocks rise on announcements or partnerships rather than orders and earnings |
| 7 | Crowding | Average pairwise correlation of the names above about 0.6. Retail options and leverage at records |
| 8 | Capacity overbuild risk | Supply growth (fabs, data centers, generation) exceeding visible demand, or utilization data missing |
| 9 | Depreciation and accounting stretch | Lengthened useful lives (for example GPUs), adjusted metrics diverging from GAAP results and cash flow |
| 10 | Breadth deteriorating | The index near highs while fewer of the theme's names stay above their 200-day average |

## Context from history

- **UK railway mania, 1840s.** Railway investment reached about 7% of GDP in 1847,
  and dividends fell to about 1.8% by 1850 versus the roughly 10% hoped for.
- **Telecom and fiber, 1996–2002.** Only about 3–5% of the fiber laid was ever lit,
  and bandwidth prices fell about 90%. Lucent, Nortel, JDS Uniphase and Corning fell
  from over $700bn combined to about $11bn.
- **Clean energy, SPACs and ARKK, 2020–21.** ARKK fell about 77–81% and was still
  about 56% below its peak five years later.
- **AI in 2025–26.** Capex was estimated at 1.2–2.3% of US GDP, at or above the
  telecom peak. There were $1.4tn+ of circular commitments, disputed GPU depreciation,
  and a sharp November 2026 drawdown in speculative AI-infrastructure stocks while
  backlogs at equipment makers kept rising.

## How to use the score

A high score does not mean "sell everything". Booms can run for years, and real
infrastructure gets built. It means:
1. Size the theme as a satellite, not a core.
2. Prefer companies with cash flow, backlog and net cash over story stocks.
3. Stage entries and pre-commit to kill criteria.
4. Expect drawdowns of 50%+ even if the thesis is right.
