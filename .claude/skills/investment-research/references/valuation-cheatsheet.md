# Valuation cheatsheet

Use more than one metric, compare against the company's own history and its peers,
and always ask what growth the price assumes.

## Core metrics

| Metric | Use for | Rough reading for large caps |
|---|---|---|
| Forward P/E | Profitable companies | Below 15 cheap, 15–25 market-like, 25–35 premium, above 60 priced for perfection |
| PEG (forward P/E ÷ growth %) | Growers | Below 1 cheap for the growth, 1–2 fair, above 3 rich |
| EV/sales | Unprofitable or early-stage companies | Above 10 needs years of 30%+ growth. Above 20 is rarely sustained |
| EV/EBITDA | Capital-heavy businesses (utilities, industrials) | Compare within the sector: utilities about 10–13, equipment 15–25 |
| FCF yield (FCF ÷ market cap) | Everything | Above 5% attractive, 2–4% fair, negative needs a funded path to profit |
| Dividend + buyback yield | Mature companies | Shows how cash goes back to owners |

## Traps

- **Cyclical peak.** Memory, semiconductor equipment, energy, materials and
  shipping look cheapest when earnings peak. In Nov 2026 Micron traded at about 3x
  forward earnings after revenue grew 379%. A very low P/E on booming sales usually
  means the market expects earnings to fall. Normalize: use mid-cycle margins times
  current revenue.
- **Adjusted versus GAAP.** Stock-based compensation is a real cost. Compare
  adjusted earnings with GAAP earnings and with free cash flow.
- **Dilution.** Check share-count growth. 5%+ a year silently cuts per-share
  returns.
- **Debt-funded growth.** Look at interest coverage, maturities and bond yields.
  High-yield spreads on a company's bonds are a market warning.
- **One-customer revenue.** A contract with one AI lab or hyperscaler can vanish
  at renewal.

## Reverse DCF in five minutes

1. Take today's market cap (or enterprise value).
2. Assume an exit multiple in 10 years, for example 20x FCF for a mature business.
3. Ask what FCF in year 10 makes a 10% yearly return:
   FCF_10 ≈ market cap × 1.10^10 ÷ 20 ≈ market cap × 0.13.
4. Compare that with today's FCF. A required 10x jump means the price assumes about
   26% yearly FCF growth for a decade. Is the market big enough, and has any company
   done it?

## Growth reality check

Revenue growth above 20% a year for 10 years is rare. Few large companies sustain
it, and almost all slow down faster than forecasts assume. Treat the consensus
5-year growth for any company above $100bn in market value as optimistic until proven.
